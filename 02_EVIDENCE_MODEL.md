# Evidence Model

Every substantive research claim in Project Atlas must carry one status.

## Observation

A directly sourced or reproduced fact about one game, artefact, event or experiment.

Examples:

- a rule implemented in source code;
- a transition reproduced through direct play;
- a dated statement by a game's creator.

A high-confidence fact about one game remains an observation.

## Pattern

A falsifiable, provisional relationship supported by at least two relevant observations. Its scope and known counterexamples must be stated.

A pattern is not confirmed merely because it sounds general or appears in several closely related clones.

## Confirmed Pattern

A scoped relationship reproduced across at least three mechanically distinct puzzle families, supported by sources and checked for counterexamples.

The threshold is a minimum, not an automatic promotion rule.

## Law

Codex and individual contributors may not assign this status. A law requires explicit human review after broader cross-family evidence and a documented decision.

## Confidence is separate

Evidence status describes the role a claim plays in the research model. Confidence describes the reliability of its support.

For example:

- exact source-code behaviour may be a high-confidence `Observation`;
- a relationship seen in two games may be a medium-confidence `Pattern`;
- an unsupported generalisation is neither a pattern nor a law.

Use the most conservative applicable status. Negative findings, uncertainty and counterexamples are valid results.
