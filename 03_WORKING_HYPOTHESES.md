# Working Hypotheses

These are provisional and must be tested.

## H-001 — Stable rules, changing situations

Long-lived puzzle games often preserve a small rule set while generating many different states or positions.

## H-002 — Present move versus future structure

Strong decisions often solve a local problem while affecting the future quality of the board.

## H-003 — Perceived agency after failure

Replay motivation is stronger when the player can attribute failure to an understandable decision rather than an opaque stat wall or payment barrier.

## H-004 — Mastery lives in the player

Durable puzzle games may provide progression mainly through improved perception and strategy rather than character statistics.

## H-005 — Action + system response + constraint

A useful decomposition of a puzzle loop is:

- player action;
- automatic system response;
- scarce resource or constraint;
- objective / evaluation.

This is a research model, not yet proven to be complete.

## H-006 — Useful patterns are discovered

Part of the pleasure may come from discovering strategies that are not explicitly taught.

## Counterexample requirement

For every hypothesis, search for:

- popular games that succeed without the property;
- games that possess the property but are not compelling;
- cases where the property exists only because of theme or presentation.
