# Research Principles

1. **Evidence before invention.** Catalogue and decompose existing mechanics before proposing finished games.
2. **Separate levels.** Do not mix elementary actions, automatic system behaviours, constraints, goals and presentation themes.
3. **Use precise language.** `Merge`, `sort`, `gravity` and `limited space` are different categories.
4. **Novelty must be checked.** Search games, papers, game-jam projects and prototypes before marking a combination as empty.
5. **Do not repeat conclusions.** New research updates must add mechanics, evidence, counterexamples, matrix entries or candidate evaluations.
6. **Keep hypotheses falsifiable.** Record counterexamples and uncertainty.
7. **Prototype ugly and early—but only after mapping.** Squares, circles and numbers are enough.
8. **Player experience outranks elegance.** A mathematically rich system can still be unreadable or boring.
9. **Failure should teach.** Prefer systems where players can understand what they did wrong.
10. **Preserve player trust.** Future designs should avoid paywalls, forced ads and punishment for absence.
11. **Compare every genome.** After decomposing a game, compare its normalised genes and core combination with every previously analysed game. Report new genes, a new combination of known genes or a full structural match. If there are no new genes, state `New genes: none`; theme, terminology and parameter changes do not create genes.
12. **Label evidence, not enthusiasm.** Use `Observation`, `Pattern` and `Confirmed Pattern` conservatively. Codex cannot assign `Law`.
13. **Report negative results.** Finding no new gene, no new combination or no taxonomy change is valid research data and must be stated directly.
14. **Make classification changes auditable.** Before correcting a taxonomy error, create a numbered `research/hypotheses/TAXONOMY_CHANGE_xxx.md` explaining the evidence and impact. Never silently rewrite the classification.
15. **Sample for diversity.** Do not analyse two structurally close games consecutively. Choose the next game for maximum expected expansion of Atlas, not because it is currently popular.
16. **Broaden the corpus.** Include classical, mathematical, mechanical, historical, experimental and academic puzzle systems alongside commercial digital games.
17. **Challenge the plan openly.** After each research unit, evaluate whether the current order still maximises expected information gain. If not, propose a concrete alternative with its criterion, expected benefit and effect on the backlog. Silence is not an acceptable plan assessment.

## Evidence ladder

`Observation → Pattern → Confirmed Pattern → Law`

- **Observation:** a directly sourced or reproduced fact about one system, event or experiment.
- **Pattern:** a falsifiable provisional relationship supported by at least two relevant observations.
- **Confirmed Pattern:** a scoped relationship replicated across at least three mechanically distinct puzzle families, with counterexamples actively checked.
- **Law:** may only be assigned through explicit human review after broader evidence. Codex may collect evidence for a possible law but may not apply the status.
